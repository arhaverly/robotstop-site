01 Why it exists

In the second that matters, nobody finds the button.

An emergency stop only works if a hand reaches one. The person who sees the pinch point first is usually the person who can’t get there — hands full, wrong side of the cell, already on the floor. What everyone does instead is shout. RobotStop makes shouting work.

A

Hands are already busy

Holding the part, holding the fixture, holding someone else back. A voice is the one input that is always free.

B

The button is somewhere else

Mushroom heads live on the cabinet and the pendant. Trouble happens at arm’s length, facing the other way.

C

Shouting is the reflex

People yell before they reach. Give that reflex a wire into the safety chain and you buy back the time it costs.

03 How it works

Two channels that don’t trust each other.

Redundancy only counts when the two halves can fail apart. RobotStop runs two complete detection paths that share no microphone, no power rail and no state — and either one of them can stop your machine. Both run the same detector, on purpose: one model, qualified once, held to one published bar.

  1. 01

    Listen

    Each channel has its own microphones and its own safety-manual microcontroller. No shared parts, and no general-purpose operating system anywhere in the stop path.

  2. 02

    Decide

    Both channels run the same detector — the one we qualify, at one operating point, against hundreds of hours of hard negatives. No wake word, no enrollment; calm or shouted, both count.

  3. 03

    Stop

    Either trigger de-energizes the outputs. The stop latches — silence does not clear it. Only a monitored rising-then-falling reset edge releases it.

  4. 04

    Prove it’s alive

    An acoustic self-test fires a test signal through the real microphone path on a schedule, and the two channels cross-check each other’s liveness. A dead mic becomes a declared fault in seconds, not a surprise on demand.

How many is “all”?

There is no definitive worldwide total for languages plus dialects. Glottolog 5.3 identifies 7,674 spoken first-language languages; its broader catalog also includes signed, historical and other categories. Our 21,944-entry reference inventory counts language and dialect records together. It is neither a census of living spoken varieties nor a count of languages RobotStop currently detects. Dialect documentation is incomplete and classifications change.

Coverage today: the development word lists cover English, Mandarin Chinese and Korean. Word lists alone do not establish qualification. The worldwide scope is an expansion program; support must be established for the model and variety you deploy.

Signed languages are part of the world’s linguistic diversity and appear in the catalog. A microphone cannot recognize signing; signed-language support would require a separate sensing approach.

    Qualification follows each variety

    Each release must name the languages, dialects and keywords it covers, with results for normal, raised and shouted speech. A parent language’s results do not qualify every dialect. Our protocol calls for at least 20 speakers and 1,000 positive utterances per acoustic condition for each variety claimed, plus confusable speech and noise testing with the full enabled language set.

    05 Versus building it yourself

    You wouldn’t build your own e-stop. Why would this be different?

    Nobody machines their own mushroom button, writes their own safety relay, or signs off on their own light curtain. They buy the part that arrives with a test report behind it. A voice stop is the same category of thing in newer clothes: the keyword spotter is a solved undergraduate project, the stop you are willing to put your name on is not, and the gap between those two is the entire product. The demo takes a weekend. The safety case takes a year.

    07 Straight about standards

    What it is, and what it is not.

    Safety products earn trust by being precise about their own limits. Here are ours, in the same words we put in the manual.

    What it is

    • A supplementary protective measure: one more stop-request input to the safety-related control system your machine already has.
    • Designed to an ISO 13849-1 Category 3 architecture, targeting PL d, so certification is possible without a redesign.
    • Frozen at release. No field learning, no drifting thresholds. Signed firmware and secure boot ship with the certified module.
    • Entirely local. No network connection, nothing outbound, no continuous audio stored. The radio on today’s module is unused and disabled in firmware; the certified part will not have one.

    What it is not

    • Not an emergency stop device in the sense of ISO 13850, and never labeled or sold as one.
    • Not a substitute for guarding, e-stops, interlocks, procedures, training or your risk assessment. It is added to them, never instead of them.
    • Not yet certified as a safety component. Units shipping today are evaluation kits, and they go out in writing as such.
    • Not a reason to stand anywhere you would not have stood before.

    Safety notice

    Keep every stop you already have.

    RobotStop is a supplementary protective measure and is not an emergency stop device under ISO 13850. It is not certified as a safety component. Nothing about installing it justifies removing, bypassing or deferring an emergency stop, a guard, an interlock, a procedure or a training requirement — it is worth having only while all of those stay in place. Voice detection can miss a word; a cable, a host or a power supply can fail outright.
